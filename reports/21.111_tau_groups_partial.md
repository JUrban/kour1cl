# Problem 21.111 (Revin–Yang) — τ-groups: partial results

**Problem.** S a finite simple non-abelian group, not ²B₂(q). A non-identity automorphism x of S is a
τ-automorphism if every two conjugates of x in ⟨x, Inn(S)⟩ generate a subgroup of order prime to 3; S is a
τ-group if it admits one. (a) List all τ-groups. (b) Do τ-automorphisms of odd order exist?

## Computational result
Exhaustive check (`work/p21_111/tau.g`, `tau2.g`, logs `run.log`, `run2.log`, `run3.log`) over all
non-abelian simple groups S of order ≤ 10⁷ with 3 | |S| (i.e. all except Sz(8)), over all conjugacy classes
of non-identity elements x of Aut(S) of order prime to 3 (an element of order divisible by 3 is never τ),
testing 3 ∤ |⟨x, x^s⟩| for every s ∈ S:

* τ-groups found: **A₆ = PSL(2,9), PSL(2,27), PSL(2,81), PSL(2,243)**, i.e. exactly PSL(2,3ⁿ), n = 2,3,4,5.
* No other simple group of order ≤ 10⁷ is a τ-group (this includes all PSL(2,q) with q ≤ 270 not a power
  of 3, A₇–A₁₀, PSL(3,q), PSU(3,q), PSp(4,q) small q, M₁₁, M₁₂, M₂₂, J₁, J₂, PSL(4,3), PSU(4,3), G₂(3), PSU(3,8), …).
* Every τ-automorphism found is an involution: an inner involution when n is odd (PSL(2,27), PSL(2,243)),
  and a diagonal (PGL-type) outer involution when n is even (A₆, PSL(2,81)).

## Theorem (the family PSL(2,3ⁿ), n ≥ 2, consists of τ-groups)
Let q = 3ⁿ, n ≥ 2, S = PSL(2,q). Let x be an involution; then for s ∈ S the group ⟨x, x^s⟩ is dihedral of
order 2·o(x·x^s), so x is τ iff no product of two conjugates of x has order divisible by 3. An element of S of
order divisible by 3 is (up to conjugacy) a non-trivial unipotent element u (the orders of elements of S are 3
and the divisors of (q±1)/2, and 3 ∤ (q±1)/2). Now u = x₁x₂ with involutions x₁, x₂ conjugate in G = ⟨x, Inn S⟩
forces x₁ to invert u, i.e. x₁ ∈ N_G(⟨u⟩).
* n odd: q ≡ 3 (mod 4), and N_S(⟨u⟩) is the Borel subgroup of order q(q−1)/2, which is odd. So no involution of
  S inverts u, and the (unique) class of involutions of S consists of τ-automorphisms.
* n even: q ≡ 1 (mod 4). In PGL(2,q) the involutions inverting u lie in the Borel subgroup of PGL(2,q),
  whose involutions are the images of diag(a,1) with a² = 1, i.e. diag(−1,1), which has square determinant
  (−1 is a square in F_q, q ≡ 1 mod 4) and so lies in PSL(2,q). Hence the outer involutions of PGL(2,q)
  (all conjugate) never invert a unipotent element and are τ-automorphisms of S.
This agrees with the computation.

## Remarks towards (a) and (b)
* By the Baer–Suzuki-type theorem of Revin (Baer–Suzuki width of the class of 3′-groups is ≤ 3, cf. 21.112),
  τ-groups are precisely the exceptions where "two conjugates generating a 3′-group" fails to imply that
  the normal closure is a 3′-group. The data suggest the answer to (a) is "S ≅ PSL(2,3ⁿ), n ≥ 2" and the
  answer to (b) is "no"; a proof would require a CFSG case analysis and is not attempted here.
* For alternating groups A_n (n ≥ 5) no τ-automorphism exists: any involution x ∈ S_n has a conjugate x′ with
  xx′ a 3-cycle (or of order divisible by 3), and for x of odd order 3 | |⟨x, x^s⟩| for suitable s.
