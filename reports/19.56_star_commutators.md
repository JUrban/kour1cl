# Problem 19.56 (Monakhov) — ⋆-commutators of coprime orders and solubility

**Problem.** A ⋆-commutator is a commutator [x, y] of two elements x, y of coprime prime-power orders. Is a finite
group G soluble if |ab| ≥ |a||b| for all ⋆-commutators a, b of coprime orders? Call this condition (M).

**Status.** Not settled. Results:
1. (Theorem) Every minimal simple group violates (M); more precisely it contains an involution a and an element b of
   odd prime order, both ⋆-commutators, with a inverting b (so |ab| = 2 < 2|b| = |a||b|). Hence every group having a
   minimal simple *subgroup* violates (M), and a counterexample to 19.56 (a non-soluble group satisfying (M)) must have
   the property that every minimal non-soluble subgroup has a non-trivial Frattini subgroup.
2. (Computation) All 208 non-soluble groups of order ≤ 2000 (orders with ≤ 5000 groups) and the groups SL(2,q)
   (q = 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 19), PSL(2,q) (q ≤ 32 and 64), Sz(8), PSL(3,3) violate (M) (`work/p19_56/check.g`, `log.txt`);
   the violating pairs are printed in the log.

## Preliminaries
(a) If H ≤ G then every ⋆-commutator of H is a ⋆-commutator of G, and (M) for G implies (M) for H. So if some
subgroup of G violates (M), so does G. Since every non-soluble group contains a minimal non-soluble subgroup G₁, and
G₁/Φ(G₁) is a minimal simple group (Thompson), a counterexample G contains a minimal non-soluble subgroup G₁; if
Φ(G₁) = 1 then G₁ is minimal simple and violates (M) by the Theorem. This proves the second sentence of item 1.
(b) In a dihedral group ⟨b, t⟩ with b of odd order and t an involution inverting b, one has [b, t] = b⁻¹b^t = b⁻²;
so if b has odd prime order s then b⁻² (a generator of ⟨b⟩) is a ⋆-commutator (b and t have coprime prime-power
orders). Similarly [b^j, t] = b^{−2j}, so every non-trivial element of ⟨b⟩ is a ⋆-commutator.
(c) In A₄ = V₄ ⋊ ⟨x⟩ (x of order 3), for y ∈ V₄∖{1}: [x, y] = x⁻¹x^y ∈ V₄ is non-trivial (x^y ≠ x since C_{A₄}(x) = ⟨x⟩);
as y runs over V₄∖{1} the commutators [x, y] run over V₄∖{1}. So every involution of A₄ is a ⋆-commutator.
(d) In a Frobenius-like group V ⋊ ⟨y⟩ with V elementary abelian and y of prime order r ≠ char acting without
non-trivial fixed points, [v, y] = v⁻¹v^y ≠ 1 for v ≠ 1, and v ↦ [v, y] is a bijection of V; so every non-trivial
element of V is a ⋆-commutator.

## Theorem. Every minimal simple group S contains ⋆-commutators a, b with |a| = 2, |b| = s an odd prime, and ab of
order 2. In particular S violates (M).
*Proof.* By Thompson's classification, S is one of PSL(2, 2^f) (f ≥ 2 prime), PSL(2, 3^f) (f odd prime),
PSL(2, p) (p > 3 prime, p ≡ ±2 mod 5), Sz(2^f) (f odd prime), PSL(3,3).

*Case S = PSL(2, q), q = 2^f ≥ 4.* The Borel subgroup is V ⋊ C_{q−1} with V elementary abelian of order q and
C_{q−1} acting fixed-point-freely on V∖{1}; pick y ∈ C_{q−1} of prime order (q − 1 ≥ 3). By (d) every element of
V∖{1} — hence every involution of S (one class) — is a ⋆-commutator. S contains a dihedral subgroup D of order
2(q+1) or 2(q−1) whose cyclic part contains an element b of order 3 (3 divides q² − 1); let t ∈ D be an involution
inverting b. By (b), b′ := b⁻² is a ⋆-commutator of order 3, and a := t is a ⋆-commutator (an involution) inverting b′,
so |ab′| = 2 < 6 = |a||b′|.

*Case S = PSL(2, q), q odd, q ≥ 5.* S contains A₄ (Dickson), so by (c) every involution of S (one class) is a
⋆-commutator. Not both (q−1)/2 and (q+1)/2 are powers of 2 (their difference is 1 and q > 3), so some odd prime s
divides (q−1)/2 or (q+1)/2; S contains dihedral subgroups of orders q − 1 and q + 1 (for q ≥ 5), so there is a
dihedral subgroup D ≤ S with an element b of order s in its cyclic part and an involution t ∈ D inverting b. As
before b′ := b⁻² and a := t are ⋆-commutators, a inverts b′, and |ab′| = 2 < 2s.

*Case S = Sz(q), q = 2^f ≥ 8.* Let P be a Sylow 2-subgroup (order q²), Z = Z(P) = Φ(P) elementary abelian of
order q, and B = P ⋊ C_{q−1} the Borel subgroup; C_{q−1} acts on Z ≅ F_q as multiplication by the elements of
F_q^×, hence fixed-point-freely on Z∖{1}. The involutions of P are exactly the elements of Z∖{1}, and Sz(q) has a
single class of involutions. By (d) (applied to Z ⋊ ⟨y⟩ with y ∈ C_{q−1} of odd prime order — q − 1 is odd and > 1)
every involution of S is a ⋆-commutator. The normaliser of C_{q−1} is dihedral of order 2(q−1); choose an odd prime
s | q − 1, b ∈ C_{q−1} of order s and an involution t inverting b; conclude as before.

*Case S = PSL(2, 3^f), f ≥ 3.* This is contained in the odd-q case above (q ≥ 27).

*Case S = PSL(3,3).* Direct computation (`check.g`): a ⋆-commutator a of order 2 and a ⋆-commutator b of order 3
with |ab| = 2 exist. (Alternatively: PSL(3,3) contains S₄ ⊇ A₄ and dihedral subgroups D₁₂ ⊇ S₃.) ∎

## Remarks on lifting through the Frattini subgroup
Let G₁ be minimal non-soluble with N := Φ(G₁) ≠ 1 and S = G₁/N. ⋆-commutators of S lift to ⋆-commutators of G₁
(a p-element of S is the image of a p-element of G₁), but the orders of the lifts are multiplied by orders of
elements of N, and coprimality and the inequality |ab| < |a||b| are not automatically preserved. For instance in
SL(2,5) the involutions of A₅ lift to elements of order 4; SL(2,5) nevertheless violates (M) (a of order 4, b of order
3, |ab| = 5). Handling all Frattini extensions of the minimal simple groups uniformly is the remaining obstacle; we
have no counterexample and no proof. A partial positive step: if some odd prime s ∈ π(S) does not divide |N|, then
(Frattini argument on the preimage of a dihedral subgroup D_{2s} ≤ S) G₁ contains an s-element b of order exactly s and
a 2-element t normalising ⟨b⟩ with b^t = b⁻¹, so b⁻² is a ⋆-commutator of order s; it remains to produce a
⋆-commutator of 2-power order inverting b, which we could not do in general.
