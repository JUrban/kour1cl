# Problem 20.21 (G. Verret / M. Conder) — partial results

**Problem.** Is there a finite group G with normal subgroups K ≅ L, both of index 12, such that G/K ≅ C₁₂ and
G/L ≅ A₄?

**Status.** Not settled. We prove structural restrictions (including Lemma T2 below) and rule out all groups of
order ≤ 2000 as well as all "local" configurations (L) with |K| ≤ 256, |K| = 512 of rank ≤ 4, and (running at the
time of writing, no candidate so far) |K| ≤ 1000 for the remaining orders divisible by 4 except 512 and 768.

## Structure of a hypothetical example
Let I := K ∩ L. Then G/I embeds in G/K × G/L = C₁₂ × A₄ as a subdirect product, and |K/I| = |L/I| (as
|K| = |L|), K/I ≅ KL/L ⊴ A₄ and L/I ≅ KL/K ≤ C₁₂ (cyclic). Hence |K/I| = |L/I| ∈ {4, 12}, K ≠ L.

*Case |K/I| = 12* (so G/I ≅ C₁₂ × A₄, K/I ≅ A₄, L/I ≅ C₁₂): let φ: L → K be an isomorphism and I′ := φ(I).
Then K has normal subgroups I ≅ I′ with K/I ≅ A₄ and K/I′ ≅ C₁₂ — the same configuration with |K| < |G|. By
descent this case is impossible (a configuration of minimal order would have I = 1 and A₄ ≅ C₁₂).

*Case |K/I| = 4*: K/I ≅ V₄ = A₄′, L/I ≅ C₄, and G/I ≅ C₁₂ ×_{C₃} A₄ ≅ C₄ × A₄ with L/I = Z(G/I) and
K/I = (G/I)′. Any element s ∈ G mapping to a 3-cycle of A₄ acts on K/I transitively on the three non-trivial
cosets and centralises L/I. Consequently the three cosets aI, bI, abI of I in K have identical element-order
statistics, and N_m(K) − N_m(I) = 3N_m(aI), N_m(L) − N_m(I) = 2N_m(uI) + N_m(u²I) (u a generator of L/I).
Moreover X := KL satisfies X/L ≅ V₄ and X/K ≅ C₄ with K ≅ L. Transporting I along an isomorphism φ: L → K gives a
normal subgroup I′ := φ(I) ⊴ K with K/I′ ≅ L/I ≅ C₄ and I′ ≅ I. Hence a necessary "local" condition is:

  (L) there is a group M (≅ K ≅ L) with normal subgroups I, I′ ≅ I such that M/I ≅ V₄, M/I′ ≅ C₄, and an
      automorphism of M of 3-power order stabilising I and acting as a 3-cycle on M/I ∖ {1}.
      (Conjugation by s induces an automorphism σ of K with σ³ acting trivially on K/I; its 3-part acts as the
      3-cycle. Note that σ's 3-part may have order 9 or more; an earlier version of the search only used
      automorphisms of order exactly 3 — the corrected search `necessary3.g` uses all non-trivial elements of a
      Sylow 3-subgroup of Aut(M).)

**Lemma (abelian M).** No abelian group M satisfies (L).
*Proof.* Induction on |M|. Let (M, I, I′, α) satisfy (L) with M abelian. The subgroup Ω₁(M) is characteristic,
so its image in M/I ≅ V₄ is α-invariant; as α acts on V₄ as a 3-cycle, this image is 1 or V₄. Since M is abelian,
Ω₁(M) ∩ I = Ω₁(I) and Ω₁(M) ∩ I′ = Ω₁(I′), and I ≅ I′ gives |Ω₁(M) ∩ I| = |Ω₁(M) ∩ I′|.
If Ω₁(M)I = M, then |Ω₁(M) ∩ I| = |Ω₁(M)|/4 = |Ω₁(M) ∩ I′|, so Ω₁(M)I′ = M and M/I′ ≅ Ω₁(M)/(Ω₁(M) ∩ I′) would be
elementary abelian, contradicting M/I′ ≅ C₄. Hence Ω₁(M) ≤ I and then Ω₁(M) ≤ I′ (same order). Now
M̃ := M/Ω₁(M), Ĩ := I/Ω₁(M) = I/Ω₁(I), Ĩ′ := I′/Ω₁(I′) satisfy (L) again (Ĩ ≅ Ĩ′ because I ≅ I′; the quotients and
the action of α are inherited), and |M̃| < |M| unless M = 1, which is impossible (M/I′ ≅ C₄). By induction no
such M exists. ∎
Direct checks also exclude M = C₄ × C₂, Q₈ × C₂, Q₈ × C₄, C₄ × C₄.


**Lemma T2 (the case I·I′ ≠ M).** There is no configuration (L) in which I I′ is a proper subgroup of M
(equivalently |I : I ∩ I′| = 2). Moreover, no special property of α is needed beyond: α ∈ Aut(M), α(I) = I, and α
permutes the three non-trivial cosets of I cyclically.
*Proof.* Induction on |M|. Write N₂(X) for the number of involutions in a subset X. Since α permutes the three
cosets aI, bI, cI cyclically and preserves element orders, N₂(aI) = N₂(bI) = N₂(cI) =: c₂, so N₂(M) = N₂(I) + 3c₂.
Let ū generate M/I′ ≅ C₄; the cosets uI′ and u³I′ consist of elements of order divisible by 4, so
N₂(M) = N₂(I′) + N₂(u²I′). As I ≅ I′, N₂(I) = N₂(I′), hence N₂(u²I′) = 3c₂.
Now I I′ has index 2 in M; I I′/I′ = I/(I∩I′) is the subgroup of order 2 of M/I′ = ⟨ū⟩, so I I′ = I′ ∪ u²I′, and
I I′ ⊇ I with index 2, so I I′ = I ∪ aI for one of the non-trivial cosets. Counting involutions in I I′ in two
ways: N₂(I) + c₂ = N₂(I′) + N₂(u²I′) = N₂(I) + 3c₂, so c₂ = 0. Hence all involutions of M lie in I, and (as
N₂(u²I′) = 0) all lie in I′. Therefore Ω₁(M) = Ω₁(I) = Ω₁(I′) =: W ≤ I ∩ I′, W ≠ 1 (M has even order), W is
characteristic, and M̃ := M/W with Ĩ := I/W = I/Ω₁(I), Ĩ′ := I′/W = I′/Ω₁(I′) is again a configuration (L)
(Ĩ ≅ Ĩ′ since I ≅ I′; the quotients M̃/Ĩ ≅ V₄, M̃/Ĩ′ ≅ C₄ and the action of α are inherited; and
|Ĩ : Ĩ ∩ Ĩ′| = |I : I ∩ I′| = 2 because W ≤ I ∩ I′). By induction (a configuration needs |M| ≥ 8) this is
impossible. ∎

*Remark.* For abelian M both cases I I′ = M and I I′ ≠ M occur, and the abelian lemma above handles both by using
the identity Ω₁(M) ∩ I = Ω₁(I). Lemma T2 shows that in any
example of Problem 20.21 (Case |K/I| = 4, with M = K, I = K ∩ L and I′ = φ(I) for an isomorphism φ: L → K) one
must have K = I·φ(I), i.e. I ∩ φ(I) has index 4 in I and K/(I ∩ φ(I)) ≅ V₄ × C₄. The remaining case
I I′ = M is not excluded by this argument (if α(I′) = I′ one can descend to (I′; I ∩ I′, ψ(I ∩ I′)) with
ψ : I → I′ an isomorphism, but the descent need not preserve α(I′) = I′).
