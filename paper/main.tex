\title{Forty-eight hours of an AI agent on the Kourovka Notebook:\\
five solved problems, several partial results,\\ and a record of the process}

\begin{abstract}
We report on a 48-hour experiment in which a general-purpose AI agent (Claude Fable 5.1 in the Claude Code
harness, with GAP~4.16.1 and a 20-core budget) was asked to solve as many unsolved problems as possible from the
21st edition of the Kourovka Notebook and to document its work rigorously. The agent triaged the roughly 2300 open
problems, launched about 190 GAP computations, and combined computer searches with short proofs. It produced
complete answers to five problems (21.32: the property ``$G\cong H'$ for some finite $H$'' is decidable, with a
quasi-polynomial witness bound; 16.60: $T(G)\ge |S_\alpha|$ for every automorphism $\alpha$; 2.78: the sets of
soluble and of absolutely simple group-theoretic numbers are finite (the main question of 2.78); 18.46: Klyachko's bound $|H|\le 2|G|^2$ for
square-root overgroups is sharp; 13.19: Gorchakov's question on subdirect products has a negative answer for
every prime), substantial partial results on seven further problems (16.14, 20.37, 20.21, 19.56, 21.26, 21.111,
18.117), and bounded-range verifications for about forty-five more. All proofs are self-contained and all
computations are reproducible from a git repository. We describe the setting, the workflow, the mistakes made
along the way, and what the experience suggests about using such agents for research mathematics. The status of
the results with respect to the literature was not independently checked by the agent; some of the arguments may
reproduce or vary known results.
\end{abstract}
